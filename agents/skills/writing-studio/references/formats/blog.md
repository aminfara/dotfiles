# Format: Blog post

A first-person post in which the author tells colleagues what they think, why, and what changes. It sets a direction and earns agreement; it is the author talking to the team, not a memo about the work.

## When to use vs. adjacent formats

- **Blog post** (this format): one author, one position, told in their own voice to a team or community. Ends with a call to action.
- **Proposal**: a decision document asking named approvers to choose. Neutral, options and trade-offs. If the reader's job is to decide, use proposal.
- **Announcement / status update**: reports what happened or is about to happen. If there is no argument to make, use those.
- **Poster / PR/FAQ**: durable orientation or working-backwards artifacts. If the reader will come back to it as a reference, use those.

Two variants; ask "is the author setting a direction for people they lead, or sharing a perspective with peers?"

- **Direction-setting** (default when the author leads the readers): what and why, a phased path, how failure is handled, what success looks like, and an ask. It sets expectations and leaves the *how* to the team.
- **Perspective**: an argument or lesson for peers. Same voice rules, looser shape: claim, story, so-what.

## Default axes

- **Purpose:** persuade and set direction (direction-setting) or share a lesson (perspective).
- **Tone:** direct, plain and warm; the author's own voice. Bold in the claim, honest about risk.
- **Medium:** internal wiki or blog. Short paragraphs, a handful of plain headings, links to evidence.
- **Audience:** colleagues who were not in the author's research or meetings. They read once, at moderate attention, and act on the ask.

## Inputs to collect

- **variant** — direction-setting or perspective. Infer from the author's role toward the readers.
- **voice samples** (optional, ask once) — two or three posts the author likes the sound of. Match their openers, sentence length, and how they introduce evidence. Without samples, use the voice rules below.

## Voice

The author speaks. Use **"I"** for what the author saw, read, tried, could not find, or concluded ("I could not find anyone who publishes this"). Use **"we"** for what the team will do or change. Address the reader as "you" in the ask.

Keep the author's working material out of the body: verification tags, confidence labels, method notes and summaries of the author's own analysis go in the notes block below the `---` divider. The body states conclusions as the author's, in sentences a colleague would say aloud.

## Template

```text
# <Title that states the position>

<Opening paragraph: the direction or claim in plain words, and who stands behind it.>

## Why
<Why this matters to this team now. One or two paragraphs, with one pointer to the evidence.>

## What changes
<The expectations, stated as outcomes and roles, not procedures.>

## How we get there
<The phased path: start small, widen on evidence, adjust as we learn. Name the shape, not the tasks.>

## When it goes wrong
<How failure is contained and what we do when it happens.>

## What success looks like
<Observable end state, with a date if there is one.>

## What I need from you
<One clear ask of the reader.>

## Evidence (optional)
<Cases or data that back the claim, at the end so they support the story without interrupting it.>
```

Headings may state the finding (relax B5); merge or drop sections the variant does not need.

## Rules for this format

- **Enforce:** A1 (the position is in the first paragraph), A2 and A8 against the actual reader, who was not in the research. Run the **reader-walk** on every paragraph (see SKILL.md Step 4).
- **Explain in sentences.** Each sentence carries a subject, a verb and, where the reader must act on it, the reason. Evidence is told as a short case with the point it supports.
- **Altitude.** Direction-setting posts state what and why and leave the how to the team; ticket keys, thresholds, per-phase mechanics and repository lists belong in a linked backing doc.
- **Numbers earn their place.** Keep a figure when it changes what the reader believes or does, and say what it measures in the same sentence.
- **Relax:** B3 (a one-paragraph hook may precede the thesis in the perspective variant); B1 for visionary claims, keeping numbers only in the evidence.
- **Watch:** telegraphic compression (ai-patterns.md #11) when the author asks for "short"; agent working-notes leaking into the body; "we" used for the author's personal research.
- **Length:** 400 to 800 words for direction-setting, evidence table excluded. "Short" means fewer points, each in full sentences.

## Quick checklist before presenting

1. Does the first paragraph state the position, in the author's voice?
2. Is every "I" the author's experience and every "we" a team change?
3. Did every paragraph pass the reader-walk?
4. Does the post stay at the requested altitude (what and why, or also how)?
5. Did you run the ai-patterns pass, including #11?
