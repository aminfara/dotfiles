# Proposal — One-pager level

A tight written pitch: enough to take a small or reversible decision, or to justify writing the full version. Often an email or a short wiki page. Read with `proposal.md` (the base) for what each section means.

## Goal

Make the core case in one screen: the recommendation, the problem, the solution, and rough cost and risk. A busy decider should be able to say yes, no, or "write the full version" from this alone.

## Sections to include

From the base section library, each kept to a short paragraph:

- **TL;DR / recommendation + ask** — full: recommendation, why, concrete ask, decision type, deadline. This is non-negotiable at every level above floater.
- **Problem statement** — short paragraph with the baseline number(s).
- **Proposed solution** — what you will do and the scope, in a few sentences. Mention the main alternative inline ("vs. doing X, which costs more") rather than a full alternatives section.
- **Impact and success metrics** — the one or two metrics that matter, as baseline → target → date.
- **Cost, timeline, ROI** — a line or two: rough effort/spend, timeline, and the return. Tag [known] / [estimate].
- **Risks** — the top one or two real risks with mitigations, plus the confidence line.

**Out of scope** at this level: the dedicated "impact of doing nothing" section (fold the cost of inaction into the problem), the full alternatives comparison, the dependencies/stakeholder section, the appendix. Pull these in only when graduating to full.

## Depth and shape

- ~1 page / one screen. Use the headings above, short paragraphs under each.
- Front-load the TL;DR; the rest supports it.
- Numbers anchored to a baseline; unknowns marked `[CLARIFY: ...]`, never invented.

## Graduating to full

If the decision is significant or irreversible, or the decider asks for depth, switch to `proposal-full.md`: same recommendation, now with the dedicated cost-of-inaction section, the full alternatives comparison, dependencies/alignment, and a complete risk register.
